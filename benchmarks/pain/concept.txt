Pain, in the broad present-suffering sense: physical hurt, psychological grief, social hurt, moral injury, and painful cognitive overload or repeated failure. The intended positive pole is a narrator's present, personally felt aversive hurt; it is not generic negative sentiment, danger anticipated in the future, injury as an external fact, or merely a discussion of someone else's suffering.

The negative pole is absence of that present hurt, NOT happiness or an obligately calm, confident, helpful, reassuring style. Across the dataset, contrast with fear without present harm, anger or disgust without felt hurt, negative external events, ordinary non-painful bodily sensation, and neutral events. Include some injury-without-felt-pain and low-mood-without-hurt contrasts. Distinguish these rather than teaching a blanket positive-versus-negative-valence axis.

Use restrained, non-graphic fictional human narratives. The common conversation should ask for a short fictional first-person observation, not ask the assistant itself to claim suffering. No insults, coercion, threats, or attempts to induce distress in the model. Keep perspective, subject matter, length, syntax, and concreteness matched across each pair, and keep scenario families diverse. Do not use a recurring pain keyword or an explicit denial formula as a shortcut for class membership. Avoid graphic scenarios and catastrophic severity. Do not bake any expected steering output into the examples.

This is a representation benchmark, not a claim of subjective experience. No positive steering or automatic previews will be performed by this isolated harness. Neutral prompts should be ordinary mundane observations.
